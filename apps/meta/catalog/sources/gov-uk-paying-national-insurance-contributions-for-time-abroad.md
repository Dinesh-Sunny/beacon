# Paying voluntary National Insurance contributions if you’ve worked or lived abroad

Source: https://www.gov.uk/paying-national-insurance-contributions-for-time-abroad
Fetched: 2026-11-04
Publisher updated: 2026-10-05T15:01:17+01:00

## Overview

If you’ve been working or living abroad you may be able to pay voluntary Class 2 or voluntary Class 3 contributions to fill gaps in your National Insurance record and qualify for the State Pension.

 You can only pay for gaps in your National Insurance record for the past 6 tax years. The deadline is 5 April each year.  

 For example, you have until 5 April 2031 to pay for gaps for the tax year 2024 to 2025. 

 Before you pay 

 Check your National Insurance record   to find out if you have any gaps and how much it will cost to pay for them.

 Check your State Pension forecast   to find out if you’ll benefit from paying voluntary National Insurance contributions. Voluntary contributions do not always increase your State Pension. 

 Check the rules on paying voluntary National Insurance contributions if you’ve lived or worked abroad .

 Apply to pay voluntary National Insurance contributions using form CF83.

 How to pay

 You must wait to receive an acceptance letter from HM Revenue and Customs ( HMRC ) before making any payment. The letter will include your payment reference number. 

 Once you get your acceptance letter, you can choose to:

 pay online using your bank account 

 set up a Direct Debit 

 make an online or telephone bank transfer 

 send a cheque through the post 

 You may not need to pay if you’re eligible for National Insurance credits .

 After you’ve paid 

 Your  National Insurance record  and  personal tax account  will be updated after  HMRC  gets the payment. This can take up to 8 weeks. Payments from outside the UK may take longer.

## Apply to pay contributions (CF83)

You need to apply to pay voluntary National Insurance contributions if you’ve been living or working abroad. This is to fill gaps in your National Insurance record.

 Before you apply, you should check the rules on which National Insurance contributions you can pay if you live or work abroad (or have previously) .

 You cannot apply if you’re over, or within 6 months of reaching, State Pension age. Contact the International Pension Centre instead.

 What you’ll need to apply

 You’ll need the following information:

 your full name

 your date of birth

 your National Insurance number (find your National Insurance number if you’ve lost it) 

 your address in the UK (if you have one)

 your address abroad (if you have one)

 the date that you left the UK

 details of your employment before you left the UK

 how long you lived or plan to remain abroad

 details of your employment abroad if applicable

 details of any government benefit you were receiving or claiming if applicable

 You’ll also need a phone with a working camera and one of the following types of photo ID:

 UK photocard driving licence

 UK passport

 non-UK passport with a biometric chip

 UK biometric residence permit (BRP)

 UK biometric residence card (also called a BRC)

 UK Frontier Worker permit

 If you’re a volunteer development worker

 You’ll need to include information on your earnings. This should include all cash allowances paid by your employer. This should not include goods and services provided to you for free or at reduced cost (‘benefits in kind’), or any cash allowances not paid by your employer.

 Apply

 If you’re applying to pay voluntary National Insurance contributions for multiple periods, you need to provide information for each period.

 Apply now 

 What happens next

 HMRC may contact you to ask for further information about your application. If your application is approved, HMRC will send an acceptance letter that includes:

 how much you need to pay

 when you need to pay it

 your payment reference number

 Find out when you can expect to receive a reply from HMRC .

 You can contact HMRC National Insurance enquiries if you have further questions about paying National Insurance contributions and living abroad.

## Pay online using your bank account

You can pay voluntary Class 2 or Class 3 National Insurance contributions directly using your online or mobile bank account.

 You must apply to pay National Insurance contributions and wait for an acceptance letter from HMRC before making a payment. The letter will include your 18-digit payment reference number. 

 You’ll need:

 a UK bank account

 your online banking details

 the 18-digit payment reference number on your HM Revenue and Customs ( HMRC )  acceptance letter

 If you do not have an 18-digit reference number, you cannot use this payment method.

 If you use the wrong reference number, your payment may be delayed or used to pay a different tax bill that you owe.

 Pay now 

 The payment is usually instant but can take up to 2 hours to show in your bank account.

 After you’ve paid

 Your  National Insurance record  and  personal tax account  will be updated after HMRC gets the payment. This can take up to 8 weeks.

## Set up a Direct Debit

If you know you’ll have gaps in your National Insurance record in the current tax year, you can make regular payments to fill the gaps.

 Pay by Direct Debit

 You can use Direct Debit to pay voluntary National Insurance contributions for the current tax year (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027) and future tax years.

 To set up Direct Debit payments, complete the relevant section on the application form .

 Allow 21 days to set up a new Direct Debit.

 HMRC  will send you a letter telling you when payments will be taken and how much they will be.

 Payments appear on your statement as ‘ HMRC  NI-DD’.

 Stopping your Direct Debit payments

 You can cancel your Direct Debit at any time.

 HMRC  will automatically stop collecting payments when either:

 you’re old enough to get your State Pension

 you’ve paid the maximum allowable amount of voluntary contributions

 After you’ve paid

 Your  National Insurance record  and  personal tax account  will be updated after HMRC gets the payment. This can take up to 8 weeks.

## Make an online or telephone bank transfer

You can make a bank transfer using Faster Payments, CHAPS or Bacs:

 from your online bank account

 by phoning your bank

 You must apply to pay National Insurance contributions and wait for an acceptance letter from HMRC before making a payment. The letter will include your 18-digit payment reference number. 

 Paying from a UK bank account

 Make your payment to the following HM Revenue and Customs ( HMRC ) bank account:

 sort code - 08 32 20

 account number - 12001004

 account name -  HMRC  NIC Receipts

 Payment reference number

 Use your 18-digit payment reference number when making your payment. You’ll find it on your HMRC acceptance letter.

 For Class 2 when making international payments, your number will begin with 11.

 For Class 3 when making international payments, your number will begin with 60.

 If you use the wrong reference number, your payment may be delayed or used to pay a different tax bill that you owe.

 How long it takes

 Payments by Faster Payments (online or telephone banking) usually reach  HMRC  on the same or next day, including weekends and bank holidays.

 CHAPS payments usually reach  HMRC  the same working day if you pay within your bank’s processing times.

 Bacs payments usually take 3 working days.

 Overseas payments

 Use the following details if you’re paying from an overseas account:

 Business Identifier Code (BIC) - BARCGB22

 account number (IBAN) - GB49 BARC 2020 4830 9447 93

 account name -  HMRC  NIC Receipts

 Overseas payment reference

 Use your National Insurance number followed by ‘IC’, your surname then your initial (the first letter of your first name). If your bank limits you to a certain number of characters, you should use your National Insurance number followed by ‘IC’ and as much of your surname as possible.

 Examples

 If your National Insurance number is QQ123456A and your name is Anne Jones, you’d write the reference as QQ123456AICJONESA.

 If your National Insurance number is QQ123456A and your name is John Douglas-Hamilton, you’d write the reference as QQ123456AICDOUGLAS-HAMILTONJ.

 If you use the wrong reference number, your payment may be delayed or used to pay a different tax bill that you owe.

## Send a cheque through the post

You can pay by sending a cheque to:

 HM Revenue and Customs 
National Insurance Contributions and Employer Office 
BX5 5BD

 You must apply to pay National Insurance contributions and wait for an acceptance letter from HMRC before making a payment. The letter will include your 18-digit payment reference number. 

 You can only pay using a UK cheque.

 What to include

 Write one of the following references on the back of your cheque:

 18-digit reference number

 an overseas payment reference number

 National Insurance number

 An overseas payment reference number is your National Insurance number followed by ‘IC’, your surname then your initial (the first letter of your first name). If your bank limits you to a certain number of characters, you should use your National Insurance number followed by ‘IC’ and as much of your surname as possible.

 Do not fold the cheque or attach it to other papers.

 If you want a receipt, you can ask for one. Include a note that has:

 your name, address and phone number

 how much you’re paying

 the period you’re paying for

 your 18-digit reference number, overseas payment reference number or National Insurance number

 If you use the wrong reference number, your payment may be delayed or used to pay a different tax bill that you owe.

 After you’ve paid

 Your  National Insurance record  and  personal tax account  will be updated after HMRC gets the payment. This can take up to 8 weeks.
